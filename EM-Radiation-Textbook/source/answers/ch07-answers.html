Chapter 7 — Color

  1. It looks nearly black. An apple's color is leftover light: its skin absorbs violet through yellow and reflects mainly red (Figure 7.3). Blue light contains no red for it to reflect, and the small fraction of blue it does reflect (a few percent) makes it only very dark, not blue.

  2. Only the red and green subpixels are lit, so the screen emits red light (around 610–630 nm) and green light (around 530 nm), with essentially no 580 nm light. That mixture excites your L cones strongly and your M cones moderately, the same ratio that 580 nm yellow light produces (Figure 7.1). Your brain receives the same three cone signals, so it reports yellow. The two spectra are metamers.

  3. \(\lambda_\text{edge} = 1240/2.7 = \mathbf{459}\) nm. ZnSe absorbs violet and the bluest part of the blue (below 459 nm) and returns everything from blue-green to red. White light minus violet and deep blue looks yellow (pale yellow), matching Figure 7.4.

  4. Diamond: \(\lambda_\text{edge} = 1240/5.5 = 225\) nm, in the UV. \(E_g \gt 3.1\) eV, so no visible photon is absorbed: transparent and colorless. Silicon: \(\lambda_\text{edge} = 1240/1.1 = 1130\) nm, in the infrared. \(E_g \lt 1.8\) eV, so every visible photon is absorbed: opaque. Silicon still looks shiny gray because its high index (\(n \approx 4\)) makes its surface reflect \(R = (3/5)^2 \approx 0.36\) of the light, roughly equally at all visible wavelengths. Equal reflection of all colors at roughly 35% looks like a neutral, metallic gray. (Silicon is transparent to infrared beyond about 1.1 µm.)

  5. \(\dfrac{I_{400}}{I_{650}} = \left(\dfrac{650}{400}\right)^4 = (1.625)^4 = 2.64^2 = \mathbf{6.97}\), so violet is scattered about 7 times more strongly than red.

  6. Air molecules are far smaller than visible wavelengths, so they follow Rayleigh's \(1/\lambda^4\) law and scatter mostly blue: the clear sky is blue. Cloud droplets (10–20 µm) are much larger than visible wavelengths and scatter all colors about equally; light also bounces among many droplets, so clouds return white light. Storm clouds are very thick: most light is scattered back out of the top or lost over many bounces, so little reaches the bottom, which looks gray from below.

  7. Pure Al₂O₃ has a band gap of about 9 eV, far above 3.1 eV, so no visible light is absorbed: sapphire is colorless. In ruby, Cr³⁺ substitutes for up to about 1% of the Al³⁺ ions. The crystal field of the surrounding O²⁻ ions splits chromium's 3d levels into gaps of about 2–3 eV, so ruby absorbs violet (near 410 nm) and yellow-green (near 550 nm) and transmits red. In blue sapphire, neighboring Fe²⁺ and Ti⁴⁺ ions transfer an electron when a photon supplies about 2 eV, absorbing yellow and orange light and leaving blue. A fraction of a percent of the right impurity sets the color.

  8. Think of white light as red + green + blue. Cyan ink absorbs red and passes green and blue. Yellow ink absorbs blue and passes red and green. Light that passes through both layers (reflecting off the paper beneath) must survive both: red is removed by cyan and blue by yellow, so only green remains.

  9. Both metals' free electrons reflect visible light strongly. The difference is where deeper d-band electrons begin to absorb. In gold, transitions from the filled 5d band into the conduction band start near 2.4 eV (about 520 nm), so gold absorbs violet and much of the blue while reflecting red, orange, yellow, and green: it looks yellow. In silver the corresponding transitions need about 4 eV (about 320 nm, ultraviolet), so silver reflects all visible colors equally and looks white or colorless. (Relativistic effects in heavy gold are why its d band sits so much closer; see the Rabbit Hole in Section 7.7.)

  10. Photon energy \(\approx E_g\): \(E_g = 1240/630 = \mathbf{1.97}\) eV, about 2.0 eV. For GaN, \(\lambda = 1240/3.4 = \mathbf{365}\) nm, in the near ultraviolet, so a GaN LED emits invisible UV light. Blue LEDs alloy indium into GaN to lower the gap to about 2.7 eV.

  11. Absorbed power = absorbed fraction × intensity × area. Black: \(0.90 \times 1000 \times 0.30 = \mathbf{270}\) W. White: \(0.30 \times 1000 \times 0.30 = \mathbf{90}\) W. The black shirt absorbs an extra 180 W, nearly twice your body's resting heat output (about 100 W), which is why it feels so much hotter.

  12. Light reflects from both the front and back surfaces of the film, and the back-surface wave travels an extra distance of about \(2nt\). Wavelengths whose crests line up are reinforced and others cancel. The film's thickness varies from place to place and changes as it drains and swirls, so the reinforced color varies: swirling bands. Just before popping, the film thins to a few tens of nanometers, much less than any visible wavelength, so the extra path is negligible. The front reflection (air into soapy water, higher \(n\)) flips the wave's phase, but the back reflection does not, so the two reflected waves are half a cycle out of step for every color and cancel. The film reflects almost nothing and looks black.
