Chapter 6 — Reflection, Transmission, Absorption, and Refraction

  1. Energy is conserved, so \(R + A + T = 1\) (Equation 6.1). With \(R = 0.06\) and \(A = 0.22\): \(T = 1 - 0.06 - 0.22 = 0.72\). The sheet transmits 72% of the light.

  2. A marble has only two surfaces in the beam's path, each reflecting about 4%, so most light goes straight through and you see clearly through it. Powder has millions of tiny surfaces at random angles. At each one, about 4% reflects and the rest refracts into a new direction. After many such encounters the light's direction is randomized and much of it comes back out diffusely. Glass absorbs no visible color, so all colors are returned equally: the powder looks white. Snow vs. ice and frosted vs. clear glass work the same way.

  3. The highlighter's dye absorbs UV or blue photons into real excited states, waits a few nanoseconds, and re-emits at a longer wavelength in a random direction with a random phase (fluorescence). Waves from different molecules have no fixed phase relationship, so they cannot add up in any particular direction: you get a glow, not an image. The mirror's electrons are driven by the wave and re-radiate immediately at the same frequency, phase-locked to it. Their wavelets reinforce only in the direction \(\theta_r = \theta_i\), so the arrangement of the incoming rays (the image) is preserved.

  4. Equation 6.4 with \(n_1 = 1.00\): diamond \(R = \left(\frac{2.42 - 1}{2.42 + 1}\right)^2 = (0.415)^2 = 0.172\), about 17%. Glass: \(R = (0.52/2.52)^2 = 0.043\), about 4%. Diamond's surface reflects roughly four times as much light as glass, which (together with its small critical angle) gives it its brilliance.

  5. Speed: \(v = c/n = (3.00\times10^{8})/1.333 = \mathbf{2.25\times10^{8}}\) m/s. Wavelength: \(\lambda = \lambda_0/n = 589/1.333 = \mathbf{442}\) nm. Frequency is unchanged: \(f = c/\lambda_0 = (3.00\times10^{8})/(589\times10^{-9}) = \mathbf{5.09\times10^{14}}\) Hz. The diver sees the same yellow. Color depends on photon energy \(hf\), and the frequency never changes when light crosses a boundary (once inside the diver's eye, the wavelength is set by the eye's fluids anyway).

  6. Snell's law: \(\sin\theta_2 = \frac{1.00 \times \sin 60.0^\circ}{1.333} = \frac{0.866}{1.333} = 0.650\), so \(\theta_2 = \mathbf{40.5^\circ}\) from the normal. The ray bends toward the normal because it enters a higher-index medium.

  7. \(\sin\theta_c = n_2/n_1 = 1.000/1.333 = 0.750\), so \(\theta_c = \mathbf{48.6^\circ}\). Light from the entire sky (every direction above the surface) is squeezed into a cone of half-angle 48.6° (about 97° across) overhead, called Snell's window. Looking up at angles outside that cone, the fish sees the underside of the surface acting as a perfect mirror (total internal reflection), reflecting the bottom and underwater scene.

  8. A metal's free electrons respond to every frequency below the plasma frequency (in the UV for most metals). Their re-radiation cancels the wave within a skin depth of tens of nanometers, reflecting 90–95% and absorbing the rest, so a thick piece transmits nothing. Gold leaf (about 0.1 µm) is about as thin as that absorption depth, so a little light, greenish for gold, leaks through. X-ray frequencies are far above the plasma frequency: the electrons cannot keep up, the metal stops acting like a mirror, and thin foils transmit X-rays.

  9. Sunlight's visible and near-infrared light passes through the glass (Table 6.1) and is absorbed by seats and the dashboard, becoming heat. The warm interior radiates mainly thermal infrared near 10 µm (Chapter 9), which glass absorbs (Si–O vibrations), so that energy cannot simply radiate back out through the windows. The closed car also traps the warm air. Energy enters faster than it leaves until the interior is much hotter than outside.

  10. The cornea's focusing comes from refraction at its front surface, and how strongly a surface bends light depends on the index jump. In air the jump is \(1.376 - 1.000 = 0.376\); in water it is only \(1.376 - 1.333 = 0.043\), about 11% as much. The cornea loses most of its roughly 43 diopters, far more than the lens can make up, so images focus well behind the retina and look blurry. Goggles restore an air layer in front of the cornea.

  11. Sapphire is a single crystal: one uniform index throughout and no internal boundaries, so light passes straight through. A spark-plug insulator is polycrystalline alumina with pores. Al₂O₃ is non-cubic and birefringent, so randomly oriented grains present index mismatches at every grain boundary, and pores (\(n\) from 1.77 to 1.00) reflect and redirect light strongly. Multiple scattering sends light back out diffusely: opaque white. Similarly, HDPE is semicrystalline: its denser crystalline regions have a different index from the amorphous regions, and the many interfaces scatter light (cloudy). PMMA is amorphous with a uniform index, so it is clear.

  12. Thin-lens equation: \(\frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{d_o} = \frac{1}{0.0500\,\text{m}} - \frac{1}{4.00\,\text{m}} = 20.00 - 0.25 = 19.75\,\text{m}^{-1}\), so \(d_i = 0.0506\,\text{m} = \mathbf{50.6}\) mm, just beyond the focal length. Magnification: \(m = -d_i/d_o = -0.0506/4.00 = -0.0127\). Image height: \(1.80\,\text{m} \times 0.0127 = 0.0228\,\text{m} \approx \mathbf{23}\) mm, inverted, which fits on a 24 mm tall sensor. Focusing on this person means moving the lens about 0.6 mm away from its infinity position.
