# Sources — Chapter 11: X-Rays and Materials Science

Note on method: live web access was limited while writing this chapter. Direct page fetches failed, and the shared web-search budget was used up during Chapter 10 research. So the Chapter 11 numbers were checked computationally against tabulated reference data (the xraydb package, which packages the Elam/Ravel/Sieber, Waasmaier–Kirfel, and Chantler tables that NIST and crystallography programs use), and against the standard textbook values listed below. All Bragg-angle, d-spacing, and planar-density arithmetic was recomputed in Python.

- X-Ray Mass Attenuation Coefficients (soft tissue and cortical bone ICRU-44 compositions; lead) — NIST (Hubbell & Seltzer) — https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/XrayMassCoef/ [values reproduced with xraydb: water 0.376, soft tissue 0.379, cortical bone 1.331, lead 30.3 cm²/g at 30 keV; lead K-edge 88.0 keV]
- xraydb: X-ray reference data in Python (line energies Cu Kα1 8.0463 keV, Kα2 8.0267 keV, Kβ1 8.9039 keV; Co Kα1 6.9309 keV; Mo Kα1 17.480 keV; W Kα1 59.318 keV; K-edges Cu 8.979, Ni 8.333, I 33.169, Ba 37.441 keV; atomic scattering factors f0) — M. Newville / Elam, Ravel & Sieber tables — https://xraypy.github.io/XrayDB/
- Materials Science and Engineering: An Introduction (Ch. 3: crystal structures, Miller indices, planar density, X-ray diffraction, Example Problem on BCC Fe (220) with λ = 0.1790 nm; atomic radii Cu 0.128 nm, Fe 0.124 nm, Al 0.143 nm, W 0.137 nm) — W. D. Callister & D. G. Rethwisch, Wiley [standard textbook reference; consistent with the reader's ME2340 course]
- X-ray diffraction and Bragg's law; X-ray tube spectra — HyperPhysics, Georgia State University — http://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/quantum/bragg.html [standard reference]
- University Physics Volume 3, §4.6 "X-Ray Diffraction" and §6.4 "X-ray production" (Bragg's law, Moseley's law) — OpenStax — https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3 [standard reference]
- Radiation Dose in X-Ray and CT Exams (chest X-ray 0.1 mSv; CT abdomen/pelvis 7.7 mSv) — RadiologyInfo.org (ACR/RSNA) — https://www.radiologyinfo.org/en/info/safety-xray [search, during Chapter 10 research]
- Lattice parameters used: Cu 0.3615 nm, Al 0.4049 nm, α-Fe 0.2866 nm, W 0.3165 nm [standard values; consistent with Callister's radii via a = 2R√2 and a = 4R/√3]
- Physical constants: h, m_e, e, c (CODATA, via the book's constants) and the relativistic electron-wavelength formula [computed: 100 kV gives 0.003701 nm, 200 kV gives 0.00251 nm]
