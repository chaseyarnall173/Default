# Book outline — who covers what (cross-reference other chapters as "see Chapter N" instead of re-teaching)

**Part I — What Light Is**
- **Ch 1 Electromagnetic Waves: Ripples in Fields** — charges and fields; what an E field and B field are;
  accelerating charges make waves; E ⟂ B ⟂ direction of travel; self-sustaining (changing E makes B and vice versa);
  no medium needed; speed c; wavelength, frequency, period, amplitude, c = λf; intensity ∝ amplitude²;
  polarization (sunglasses, LCD screens); antennas (Wi-Fi, radio); energy carried by the wave.
- **Ch 2 Photons: Light as Packets of Energy** — why a pure-wave model fails; photoelectric effect (threshold
  frequency, work function, KE_max = hf − φ, why brighter red light can't eject electrons); E = hf = hc/λ;
  the 1240 eV·nm shortcut; what a photon "is" (quantum of the EM field, no mass, not made of smaller parts,
  momentum p = h/λ); intensity = photon count × energy per photon; wave–particle duality (how both pictures are
  true and when to use which); photon counts from everyday sources.
- **Ch 3 The Electromagnetic Spectrum** — tour radio → microwave → IR → visible → UV → X-ray → gamma:
  wavelength / frequency / photon energy ranges, size comparisons, natural & artificial sources, uses,
  what each band typically interacts with in matter (preview of Ch 5–11), atmospheric windows,
  "visible light is a tiny slice", why our eyes see 400–700 nm.

**Part II — Atoms and Energy Levels**
- **Ch 4 Inside the Atom: Scale, Orbitals, and Quantized Energy** — atom size (~0.1–0.3 nm), nucleus size (fm),
  marble-in-a-stadium scale; atoms are mostly empty space (and why matter still feels solid);
  electron as probability cloud / orbital; why energy is quantized (standing-wave picture);
  hydrogen energy levels (−13.6 eV/n²); ground vs. excited states; ionization energy defined.
- **Ch 5 Absorption and Emission: Electrons Jumping Up and Down** — photon absorbed only if energy matches
  a gap (resonance); emission when electrons drop; ΔE = hf; spectral lines & fingerprints (neon signs, fireworks,
  star composition); continuous vs. line spectra; what happens to absorbed energy (re-emission, heat via
  collisions, chemistry); fluorescence, phosphorescence, glow-in-the-dark; molecules: vibrational (IR) and rotational
  (microwave) levels; solids: energy bands, band gap, metals vs semiconductors vs insulators; LEDs; lasers (brief).

**Part III — When Light Meets Matter**
- **Ch 6 Reflection, Transmission, Absorption & Refraction (Optics)** — the four fates (R + A + T = 1, + scattering);
  the microscopic picture: the wave shakes electrons, shaken electrons re-radiate; reflection as coherent
  re-radiation (law of reflection, specular vs diffuse); why metals are shiny (free electrons, plasma frequency);
  why glass is transparent (band gap > visible photon energy) and why it's opaque to UV/IR;
  refractive index & "light slowing down" (superposition, NOT absorb-and-wait); Snell's law; total internal
  reflection & fiber optics; dispersion & prisms/rainbows; lenses & focusing basics; opacity vs translucency
  (scattering from pores/grain boundaries in ceramics).
- **Ch 7 Color** — color = which wavelengths reach your eye; the eye's three cones; additive vs subtractive mixing;
  mechanisms of color: selective absorption by molecules (dyes, chlorophyll), band gaps in semiconductors &
  minerals (the 1.8 eV / 3.1 eV rule), metals (gold, copper vs silver), impurity/transition-metal ions (ruby,
  sapphire), scattering (blue sky, red sunset, white clouds), structural color (soap bubbles, butterflies), emission
  (neon, LEDs, screens), why black things get hot and white things don't.
- **Ch 8 Interference and Diffraction (and Why Size vs. Wavelength Matters)** — superposition; constructive /
  destructive interference; Huygens' principle; single slit, double slit, gratings (CDs); diffraction as a wave
  property (and electron diffraction → quantum: matter waves, λ = h/p); the diffraction limit & resolution
  (~λ/2), why you can't see atoms with visible light, electron microscopes; size vs wavelength rules
  (scattering ∝ 1/λ⁴ for tiny particles, microwave oven door mesh, radio waves bending around buildings) AND why
  atoms 5000× smaller than visible wavelengths still absorb/emit it (resonance, not size match);
  antennas; Project Hail Mary: Astrophage and its infrared wavelength.
- **Ch 9 Infrared, Heat, and Thermal Radiation** — heat vs temperature vs radiation; every object above 0 K emits;
  blackbody radiation; Wien's law & Stefan–Boltzmann; you glow at ~9–10 µm; red-hot/white-hot metal and color
  temperature; why IR is called "heat radiation" (molecular vibrations absorb it strongly; dominant emission at
  everyday temps) and why that's only half true (visible light heats too); thermal cameras; greenhouse effect;
  microwave ovens; emissivity; thermos/space blankets; radiative cooling in space.

**Part IV — Radiation**
- **Ch 10 Ionizing Radiation, Shielding & Space** — "radiation" means two things (EM vs particle); ionizing threshold
  (~10 eV, far-UV); why non-ionizing light can't ionize no matter how bright; UV & sunburn (photochemistry);
  alpha, beta, gamma, neutrons, cosmic rays; how radiation damages cells/DNA; dose units (Gy, Sv), everyday doses;
  inverse-square law; shielding (lead, water, polyethylene, mass, hydrogen-rich materials); magnetic fields deflect
  charged particles (Lorentz force, Earth's magnetosphere, Van Allen belts, aurora) but not photons;
  space radiation & spacecraft shielding; Project Hail Mary: radiation concerns (tunnel between ships).
- **Ch 11 X-Rays and Materials Science** — how X-rays are made (bremsstrahlung + characteristic Kα lines,
  Cu Kα = 0.1542 nm); X-ray imaging ("X-ray vision": why bones block and flesh doesn't, Z dependence);
  why X-ray wavelengths match atomic spacing; X-ray diffraction & Bragg's law nλ = 2d sinθ;
  d_hkl = a/√(h²+k²+l²) for cubic; reading a diffraction pattern; selection rules for BCC/FCC;
  parallel planes (110) vs (220) and higher-order reflections; interplanar spacing vs planar density;
  worked Callister-style problems (BCC Fe (220) with λ = 0.1790 nm); electron microscopes & diffraction.
